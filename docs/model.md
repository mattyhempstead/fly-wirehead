# Matrix model and operation

[Back to README](../README.md)

## Presentation and shared input

The `matrix` branch renders one detailed room of 96 flies and 96 phones in 8 columns and 12 rows. At 5.6 × 3.75 units per station, its occupied footprint is 44.8 × 45 units, inside a 64 × 64 room. There is one Python worker and one native connectome.

The room sits inside a 10 × 10 block of rooms spaced 70 units apart. A second 10 × 10 grid repeats those blocks at 830-unit spacing, with 96-unit roads between their 734-unit plinths. Each plinth has a low perimeter light, making the grouping legible without adding tall walls. The total is 100 blocks and 10,000 rooms. This represents 960,000 flies visually, with 96 independently timed source feeds in the detailed room. It does not create additional brains or individual playheads for distant copies. A 1024 × 1024 half-float render target captures the real room from above, with mipmaps for stable minification. Up to 81 nearby rooms add instanced fly models, raised benches, pads, and screens using the live source atlas. The lightweight flies preserve the original body proportions, faceted red eyes, antennae, six jointed legs, head sockets, and swept wing outlines. Two additional instanced draws render their bodies and wings, with no per-frame transform uploads. The nearby tier fades into the room image across spans 450–750 as the view widens; far rooms use a textured floor and a simple wall shell above the shared block plinths and foundation. Both detailed and repeated room walls are six units high, down from twenty.

All room and block instances are created at startup. They are ordered in concentric rings so a padded drawing neighbourhood can expand ahead of the camera. The drawing margin includes raised walls and low viewing angles, keeping geometry ready before it reaches the frame. The primary 100-room block and the other 99 blocks use separate batches. Neighbouring rooms fade in across visible spans 28–110, replacing the later, shorter 65–95 interval. Panning into another room keeps the neighbouring tier visible when zooming in: the fade also considers the camera target projected onto the floor. The outer grid is fully opaque from the start, with no reveal threshold or fade-in. The expanding camera frame alone reveals the surrounding blocks; they can already be seen around the edges of the 100-room view. At the largest scale, all 10,000 rooms and 100 plinths use seven draw calls. The original room keeps its complete animated geometry until a span of 4,000 units, near the end of the pullback. This avoids the earlier visible replacement at span 180; the room occupies only about a dozen pixels in the normal preview before its proxy takes over. Detailed fly posing then stops until the room is visible again. Its screen atlas and shared neural observations continue. Copies share the source imagery; the large scene is a rendering demonstration, not 960,000 independent simulations.

`prepare_matrix.py` decodes the twelve prepared MP4s into 144 × 256 frame sheets at 15 fps. Clip duration is bounded by the available decoded frames; the previously trimmed four-second clip cannot reveal later footage. These local WebP sheets are drawn into one 8 × 12 screen atlas. Each station has a deterministic independent timer, clip order, phase, and start offset. The timer advances with visible, unpaused presentation time. Phone slides and foreleg poses share the same 900 ms transition function.

Each fly receives a seeded Fisher–Yates shuffle of the complete collection. Duplicate cycles are rejected, including rotations of the same cycle, so the twelve-video collection produces 96 distinct loops. Each loop plays all twelve clips once before repeating; playback offsets and swipe timers remain independent. The seed keeps a fly's order stable across reloads.

The Three.js screen geometry maps each phone to its own atlas tile. Phones face along −X toward flies facing +X. Repeated bodies, wings, benches, swiping limbs, and wires use instancing; the entire screen atlas is rendered as one mesh. Observer camera controls do not affect the sensory image.

Each head wire rises vertically from the animated crown socket to a supply line at height 2.85. Eight thin lines run along Z directly above the heads, meeting an X-axis collector at the rear of the room. All routing after the head rise stays in that horizontal plane. The old tall connector stalks, transverse crossbars, and floor-mounted rail supports are removed. Junction lights follow the new routing, which is also captured in the distant room imagery.

Each fly's anatomical back right leg (+Z, hind pair) carries a metal cuff aligned with the lower leg. Nineteen alternating metal links drape over the bench edge to an eyelet bolted to the factory floor. The cuff follows the fly's body transform; small movements taper through the upper links while the lower chain and anchor remain fixed. This is visual restraint choreography, not a chain physics solver. The original body proportions, compound eyes, antennae, and wing shape are retained, with finer body facets, bristles, and cross-veins.

The containment environment adds green wall-top trim, green station lights, and slow floor mist. These decorative effects advance on the presentation clock and freeze with the floor. Emission varies slowly within a small range, with no abrupt flashing. The haze, lighting, vignette, and scanlines do not alter the phone atlas or the pixels sent to the shared brain. The phone footage remains in its original color.

For each observation, the browser copies one phone's currently composited tile into a 90 × 160 canvas, flips the RGBA rows into the native WebGL-compatible order, and submits it through the existing local bridge. Selection advances round-robin across all 96 stations. The brain therefore receives successive samples from different phones, not a simultaneous 96-image retinal field. There are no individual neural memories or feedback loops per station. The UI's current input station identifies the sampled display.

The browser submits nothing before the cache is ready, after rendering fails, while hidden, or while the floor is paused. An already accepted observation can finish. The visual floor can continue during brain loading or disconnection; actual measurements are then unavailable and displayed as a dash.

## Neural dynamics

The retained MaleCNS graph has 166,700 neurons and 25,582,938 directed connections. The inferred visual projection supplies 3,335 R1–R6 cells and 811 R8 cells from display luminance and color. Python calls the C++17 kernel through `ctypes`, integrating 50 ms per accepted observation in 0.1 ms steps by default.

Video-linked stimulation remains enabled by default: every accepted phone observation supplies 20 mV-equivalent current to the 15 annotated PAM11 cells. Use `--no-video-reward` with its own run directory for a control. The existing manual stimulus API remains available, although the factory UI does not expose a stimulus button or dopamine chart.

The experimental plasticity rule can modify 7,835 existing KC→MBON07/11 connections. The UI shows the real latest whole-network spike count. Measured MN9/DNp09 activity modulates wing flutter, with an independent presentation phase per fly. Every 3D fly has a deterministic wingbeat phase, frequency, and amplitude seeded by its room and station, so the same station in two rooms does not flap in sync. Frequencies range from about 2.9–3.8 cycles per second, with a roughly 5.8–24.4° sweep depending on the fly and shared measured motor response. Detailed flies use CPU joint transforms; the lightweight copies rotate wing vertices and normals around their shoulder pivots on the GPU. Both use the same presentation clock and stop while paused. At campus scale, the existing room images replace individually animated geometry. Swiping, idle movement, and clip selection remain choreographed. All 96 models share the same measurements. These mappings establish neither learned preference nor realistic physiology.

## Controls and persistence

The buttons below the scene select the factory, a row, one station, 100 rooms, or all blocks; pause the floor; and toggle fullscreen. A second toolbar selects Orbit or Pan as the drag mode, provides four directional pan buttons, and recenters the view. Shift-drag always pans. Arrow keys pan while the scene has keyboard focus; scroll zooms, Space pauses, C changes view, and F toggles fullscreen. Reduced-motion preferences start the floor paused and omit the swipe gesture and sliding transition.

The simulation canvas maintains a 16:9 aspect ratio at every page width. In fullscreen it fits inside the display with letterboxing. Station and block labels are removed from the scene; camera controls and the shared neural readout remain outside the image.

Dragging grabs the scene in both axes and stops immediately on release. Sensitivity follows the canvas height and decreases in close views and at higher zoom. A drag interrupts a preset transition at the currently displayed pose; selecting another preset turns along the shortest arc and smoothly restores its zoom. Orbit tilt stays bounded without flipping over.

Panning translates the camera target in its screen plane, using the rendered orthographic span, zoom, and viewport dimensions to match pointer motion in pixels. It preserves the camera angle and zoom, works while paused, and takes over an active reveal at the displayed pose. Recenter restores the target saved before the first pan; selecting a preset or restarting the reveal clears that anchor. Room visibility includes the target's vertical displacement so panning does not incorrectly hide blocks under the viewing ray.

The Play reveal button below the scene starts a 30-second camera sequence: a 1.4-second hold on a central fly followed by one continuous pullback through the room, a 10 × 10 room block, and a 10 × 10 grid of those blocks. Station G5 is one of the four nearest the room's centre; the original room and its block also occupy central positions in their respective even-sized grids. The opening saves the user's chosen angle and panned target, with the same visible vertical span adapted to the wider frame. Single station opens this saved shot too. Elevation gradually changes from about 37° above horizontal at the opening to 27° in the wide view, with minimal rotation and recentering as the span expands. Logarithmic zoom speed is constant through the middle of the sequence, including both grid reveals. Integrating a smooth speed ramp over the first and last 6% of travel gives gentle acceleration and deceleration without an intermediate stop. The camera's distance and clipping range grow with the view, and the final framing fits the whole grid on both portrait and landscape screens. The sequence ends at the All blocks preset and can be replayed or restarted. Pause holds its clock; dragging, scrolling, or choosing a preset cancels it at the displayed pose. Starting it resumes a paused floor, including when reduced-motion preferences initially paused playback. It runs only after an explicit button or WebMCP `reveal` action; it never autoplays.

`runs/matrix/brain.npz` retains neural state and plastic weights. It is independent of `runs/local` and `runs/recovery`. Checkpoints save every two active minutes, on a WebMCP save request, and on Ctrl-C. An explicitly supplied `--run-dir` overrides the default. `--fresh` starts over in that directory and will replace its checkpoint when saved.

`events.jsonl` contains actual measurements and image/spike hashes; `latest-input.png` holds the most recent phone pixels; `latest.json` contains the last result. One browser window owns sensory submission at a time. A second may take over after four seconds without input from the first. The HTTP server binds to loopback and validates local origins and session tokens.

## Validation

JavaScript checks cover the complete grid, independent timers, diversity of clip selection and swipe phases, uninterrupted playheads within turns, the four-second trim, shared phone/leg easing, pause, and native pixel orientation. Existing checks retain neural bridge behavior and measured-rate preservation. Browser checks exercise the actual factory renderer, all 96 feeds, shared live telemetry, camera controls, and pause/resume.

Full-network assays in `tests/test_full_connectome.py` validate black/white visual responses, stimulated/control trials, frozen plasticity, and exact checkpoint restoration. Matrix changes the presentation and input routing, not the neural equations or kernel.
